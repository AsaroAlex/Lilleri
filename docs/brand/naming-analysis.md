# Naming analysis: "Lilleri"

**Project:** LILLERI (greenfield consumer PFM, Italy-first then Europe).
**Document type:** professional naming analysis (brand strategy + type/naming specialist), synthesised from `docs/research/raw/naming-lilleri.md` and a web-search closing pass.
**Verification date for every claim:** 2026-10-02.

> **This document is not a legal trademark clearance.** Nothing here replaces a professional clearance search and a written opinion from IP counsel (EUIPO, UIBM, WIPO, UK IPO, Swissreg; classes 9, 35, 36, 38, 42). It assesses the name as a *brand asset* and records what is known, assumed and unknown about its availability.

**Verdict in one line:** the name stays **Lilleri** (GO), conditional on a professional clearance search and on registering the domain shortlist; no serious blocker was found in any accessible channel.

## 1. Meaning: what "lilleri" is, and what that does for the brand

| # | Claim | Status | Evidence (verified 2026-10-02) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | "Lilleri" is a lemmatised headword in the **Vocabolario del fiorentino contemporaneo** (Accademia della Crusca), glossed as 'denari, soldi contanti' (money, cash), marked dialectal and humorous | FACT (medium: the Crusca page exists at the cited URL and its gloss is quoted in the search snippet; the page was not opened) | WB-N1 |
| 1.2 | The singular "lillero" is in the Olivetti Italian dictionary: regional Tuscan, 'denari contanti, quattrini', used mostly in the plural; the entry attributes the origin to *tallero* (the thaler coin) | FACT (medium) for the gloss; the *tallero* etymology is the dictionary's note, in conflict with the "expressive/onomatopoeic" origin the raw memo recalled → etymology recorded as **two candidate accounts, both ASSUMPTION** | WB-N2 |
| 1.3 | The proverb **"Senza lilleri 'un si lallera"** (Florentine; standard rendering "senza lilleri non si lallera") = "without money nothing gets done / no money, no party"; "lallerare" = to sing la-la, to make merry, to spend | FACT (multiple independent attestations: Crusca, Virgilio, Toscana Inside, corpus hits in the raw memo) | WB-N1, WB-N3, raw S7–S11 |
| 1.4 | Register: familiar, affectionate, slightly ironic; never vulgar | ASSUMPTION (consistent across all attestations: political communiqué, startup blog, children's craft product, proverb explainers) | raw §1.1f |
| 1.5 | Regional reach: strongly Tuscan; outside Tuscany known mainly through the proverb (TV, comedy, social media); recognition rate elsewhere UNKNOWN | ASSUMPTION; **HYPOTHESIS to test:** ≥ 50 % of Italians aged 25–45 outside Tuscany recognise the proverb when prompted (Low 30 % / Base 50 % / High 70 %; no data) | raw §1.1e |
| 1.6 | A Tuscan community barter market in Capannori (Lucca), "Lillero – Il vero mercato del baratto", used "lilleri" as its unit of exchange from 31 March 2019 until it closed in December 2025 | FACT (medium: shop site and two local outlets) | WB-N4 |

**Brand reading.** The name literally says *money* in an affectionate, non-corporate Italian voice, and it comes with a proverb that already lives in Italian pop culture. Three consequences for the brand:

1. **Instant warmth without cuteness** — the word is adult (a proverb word, not a nursery word) but light; it does the opposite of "Bank", "Pay", "Money", "Fin-".
2. **A ready-made story for onboarding and PR** ("Lilleri is Tuscan for money; the proverb says nothing gets done without it; the app makes sure you always know where yours is") — a story, not a tagline.
3. **A pun to use with restraint.** "Lallerare" means to have fun *and* to spend; flipping the proverb into "con Lilleri si lallera" would promise fun/spending, which contradicts a trust-first positioning. The flip Lilleri can own is about *order and knowing*, not partying (see `messaging-framework.md`, candidate #24). DECISION: the proverb appears in brand storytelling and Tuscan-market campaigns only, never in UI or in the master tagline.

### 10.3 Absolute-grounds flag: a word that means "money"

Because "lilleri" means *money* in Tuscan, an EUTM or Italian application in class 36 could face a descriptiveness or lack-of-distinctiveness objection (Art. 7(1)(b)/(c) EUTMR; Art. 13 CPI). **HYPOTHESIS: risk low-to-moderate** (dialectal, not understood nationwide, used playfully), but a Tuscan examiner is a realistic scenario. Mitigations: file a word mark *and* a figurative mark; lead with classes 9 and 42 (software), where the objection is weaker; gather evidence of distinctive use from launch. Indicative probability of an absolute-grounds objection on the class-36 word mark (ASSUMPTION): Low 10 % / Base 25 % / High 40 %. **Counsel must assess.**
