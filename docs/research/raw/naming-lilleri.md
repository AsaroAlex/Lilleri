# Naming analysis: "Lilleri" (preliminary, not a legal clearance)

**Scope note.** This document is a preliminary naming analysis of the official product name "Lilleri" for the LILLERI personal-finance app (Italy-first, then Europe). It covers (1) meaning and etymology, (2) existing usage of the name, (3) domains, (4) social handles, (5) trademark landscape, (6) linguistic qualities, and ends with a risk assessment and a verification checklist. It does **not** propose alternative names (no serious legal or commercial blocker was found) and it is **not** a legal trademark clearance. Every variable claim is labelled FACT / ASSUMPTION / HYPOTHESIS / UNKNOWN with source, verification date (2026-10-02) and reliability.

## 3. Domains

Method: live DNS lookups (A, NS, MX, TXT, SOA) from the sandbox resolver on 2026-10-02. WHOIS and every RDAP endpoint (Verisign, rdap.org, Google Registry, nic.it) were blocked, so **registrant, creation and expiry dates are UNKNOWN**. A domain with no A record may still be registered (parked without DNS); a domain with DNS records is certainly registered.

**[adv-pass] Method upgrade (2026-10-02).** The adversarial pass added **NS, SOA, MX and TXT lookups** with dnspython against the public resolvers 8.8.8.8 and 1.1.1.1 (S30). An **NXDOMAIN** answer to the NS query means the name is not delegated in the TLD zone at all. For the gTLDs and the European ccTLDs tested, a registered domain is almost always delegated, so NXDOMAIN is a **strong — but not conclusive — indication that the name is unregistered** (edge cases: registered with no nameservers, registry "serverHold", or a very recent registration not yet in the zone). WHOIS over TCP port 43 (Verisign, nic.it, EURid) timed out and every RDAP endpoint was still denied, so registrant data and the registered-but-undelegated edge case remain unverified. Plain-HTTP fetches of lilleri.com / lilleri.it / lillery.com answered `403 x-deny-reason: host_not_allowed` from the proxy, i.e. the site content is still unseen.

### 5.3 Absolute-grounds flag: descriptiveness of a word meaning "money"

Because "lilleri" means "money" in Tuscan, there is a non-trivial question whether an EUTM or Italian application in class 36 (financial services) could face an objection for descriptiveness or lack of distinctiveness (Art. 7(1)(b)/(c) EUTMR; Art. 13 Codice della Proprietà Industriale). HYPOTHESIS: the risk is **low-to-moderate** because the word is dialectal/regional and not understood by the average Italian consumer nationwide, and because it is used in a playful, non-literal way; but EU and Italian practice can treat regional words as descriptive when a non-negligible part of the relevant public (here, Italian speakers) understands them, and a Tuscan examiner is a realistic scenario. Mitigations: file as a stylised wordmark/logo as well as a word mark; gather evidence of distinctive use; consider classes 9 and 42 (software) where the objection is weaker. **Counsel must assess.**
