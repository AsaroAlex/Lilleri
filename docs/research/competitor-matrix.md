# Competitor matrix: PFM apps, neobank analytics and shared-expense apps relevant to Lilleri

**Project:** LILLERI (greenfield consumer PFM, Italy-first then Europe)
**Date / verification date for every claim:** 2026-10-02
**Author:** Product Manager (consumer fintech) + growth lead + UX researcher, founding team
**Status:** synthesis of Phase 0 research; companion to `market-analysis.md` and `opportunity-map.md`

## How to read this document

- **Rows** are products; **columns** are the fields requested in the brief (target, markets, onboarding steps-to-value, bank connection/aggregator, sync, categorisation/AI learning, rules, automation, recurring, merchant normalisation, review flow, splits, transfers, refunds, cash, budget, forecast, net worth, goals, notifications, anomalies, shared, receipts, import/export, web, pricing, free tier, ads/affiliate, strengths, weaknesses, recurring complaints). A 35-column table does not render, so the wide matrix is split into **four blocks that share the same row order**: A (identity and commercial), B (categorisation and reconciliation), C (planning, collaboration and data), D (assessment). Read a product across the four blocks.
- **Labels inside cells:** `[F]` FACT, `[A]` ASSUMPTION, `[H]` HYPOTHESIS, `[U]` UNKNOWN. Reliability of the underlying source is given where it matters (high/medium/low). **UNKNOWN is used wherever the raw research found nothing**; it is never replaced by a guess.
- **Sources:** each cell points to the raw research note and section that holds the evidence and its URL: `US-§2.1` = `docs/research/raw/competitors-us.md` section 2.1; `EU-§3.6` = `competitors-eu-uk.md`; `IT-§2.4` = `competitors-italy-and-ai-first.md`; `PP-§A` = `user-pain-points.md`; `BR-§2E` = `brand-competitor-analysis.md`; `NEW-n` = WebSearch run on 2026-10-02 for `market-analysis.md` §12.3. Full URL tables are reproduced in those files; the ones cited directly here are listed in §6.
- Prices are in the currency the vendor shows; all were seen as search snippets on 2026-10-02 and must be re-read on the vendor page before use. The raw notes' adversarial passes resolved several conflicts (e.g. Emma £5.99 = extra-member price; PocketGuard free tier removed; Monarch Plus $199 vs $299 still in CONFLICT).
- Row groups: 1 US-centric PFMs · 2 UK PFMs · 3 continental-EU PFMs and global trackers · 4 banks and neobanks used in Italy · 5 shared-expense apps · 6 Italian-born PFMs · 7 platforms and open source.

## 5. Per-product verdicts (one paragraph each)

**Wallet by BudgetBakers.** The de-facto automatic tracker for Italian finance creators because it is the one that links Fineco, BBVA Italia and Revolut through Salt Edge, auto-categorises most transactions and accepts rules for unrecognised local merchants (FACT, low-medium, IT-Y-02/Y-03). Its data model is the best public reference for first-class transfers (locked Transfer category, pair/unpair/orphan states, cross-currency pairs; FACT, IT-D-03), yet it is also the clearest warning: a 44% one-star Trustpilot distribution, "sync no longer working since December 2024", "support totally non-existent" and balances without transactions (FACT, low-medium, EU-S-38). Pricing in Italy is €5.99/€17.99/€29.99 with unlabelled periods (FACT on amounts). Verdict: Wallet is both the incumbent to displace in the S1/S2 segment and the proof that coverage without reconciliation quality and support does not hold trust.

**Spendee.** A visually praised, Italian-localised tracker with shared wallets and multi-currency, sold at Plus $1.99 / Premium $5.99 (doubled in 2026; FACT, EU-S-75). Its Italian reviews describe three weeks to connect a bank, advertised banks that no longer connect, six weeks without data and forced logouts (FACT, low-medium, EU-S-13a). Verdict: a source of S2 refugees and a benchmark for shared-wallet UX, not a durable competitor; its weaknesses map one-to-one onto Lilleri's connection-health and support requirements.

**YNAB.** The method-driven benchmark for trust mechanics: a 34-day trial without card, approve-and-match import semantics with a 10-day manual-vs-import window, deterministic import IDs, a public API and Trustpilot 4.6 on 3,103 reviews (FACT, US-§2.1, US-W6). It is also the opposite of Lilleri's promise: automation is low by design, learning is payee-memory only, pending items stay out of the plan until cleared, and credit-card reconciliation is a documented chore. YNAB has run Plaid-based Direct Import in Italy since 5 Feb 2025 (FACT, high) but bank-level Italian quality is UNKNOWN. Verdict: not a competitor for S1 in Italy; copy its portability and matching semantics, not its workload.

**Monarch Money.** The post-Mint winner: $100M ARR, 1M members, $850M valuation and a move "beyond insight into action" via the MBI acquisition (FACT, US-W11/W12/W13). Its rules-driven review status (needs review / reviewed, assignable to a household member, splitting counts as review), unlimited household members and native receipt extraction are the best-in-class references for Lilleri's Review Inbox and household model (FACT, US-S28/S31/S40). Its failures are equally instructive: hidden pending transactions that reappear as new posted entries, duplicates after re-linking, undocumented transfer pairing and a 7-day trial that is "too short to finish setup" (FACT, US-S29/S23/S39). US/CA only; Plus at $199–$299/yr (CONFLICT). Verdict: the product benchmark for review flow and household; irrelevant as a competitor in Italy.

**Copilot Money.** The categorisation benchmark: a separate ML model per user trained on that user's reviewed transactions, active after ~30 reviews, surfacing the top two guesses so correction is one tap; typed transactions (Regular / Income / Internal Transfer) with both legs of a card payment excluded from spend (FACT, high, US-S51/S52/S53). Weaknesses: Apple-only (no Android; web only since Dec 2025), US-only, single-user, and "clear local cache" as the official fix for lingering pending copies and account duplicates (FACT, US-S54/S55/W15). Verdict: re-implement the per-user learning and the typed-transaction model originally, add the "why" Copilot lacks, and never ship its cache-clearing failure mode.

**Rocket Money.** A mass-market US freemium app whose real product is subscription detection and cancellation, now extended by Rowan, an SMS-first agent built with Anthropic models that cancels, negotiates and saves (FACT, US-S70/S71). Its revenue grew +$42M in H1 2026 from paying subscribers (FACT, high, 10-Q) while its trust scores collapsed on billing practices (Trustpilot 3.5, BBB user average 1.89, charges after cancellation, surprise 35–60% negotiation fees; FACT medium / ASSUMPTION for BBB). Reconciliation is manual ("Internal Transfer" set by the user) and duplicates from PayPal plus bank are solved by "ignore". Verdict: the strongest evidence that subscription detection is a loved job and that dark-pattern billing destroys trust; a pattern library for what to build and what to avoid, not a competitor.

**Emma.** The UK freemium reference with >3 million users and profitability on £5.7M revenue (FACT, medium, EU-S-64/S-65), monetised through cashback, affiliate trials, invest AUM and interest margin. It officially learns after three similar edits yet is reviewed as never learning; custom categories and rules sit behind the £9.99 Pro tier; trial auto-charges are the top complaint (FACT, EU-S-20/S-21). It explicitly cannot connect banks outside the UK, US and Canada (FACT, high, EU-S-19/S-63). Verdict: not an Italian competitor; a monetisation and paywall benchmark whose gating of corrections is the anti-pattern Lilleri must avoid.

**Snoop.** A free UK insight feed (Vanquis-owned since July 2023; Tink aggregator) with switching commissions as its main revenue and a Plus tier now at £5.99/£47.99 (FACT, EU-S-70/S-71). Categorisation accuracy is its recurring complaint ("sums bore no relation to reality"; one-offs counted as regular). Verdict: UK-only; useful as a benchmark for a nudge feed and for aligned affiliate monetisation, and as a warning that recurring detection must demand periodicity evidence.

**Plum.** The only UK brand already in Italy (since January 2023) and profitable since January 2026 with £34M ARR and £3.1bn AUM (FACT, EU-S-27/S-67); it is a savings and investing app with light PFM, priced in Italy at Pro €3.99 / Premium €9.99 (FACT, high, EU-S-66). Its Italian App Store rating of 3.4/5 is dominated by "cannot link or change bank" and withdrawals blocked by link problems (FACT, medium, EU-S-11). Verdict: a direct Italian precedent that bank-linking quality decides reviews; a potential partner-model reference (three aggregators for resilience), not a PFM competitor.

**MoneyWiz.** A power-user desktop-plus-mobile app that reaches Italy through Salt Edge Partner, with Premium at $4.99/mo or $49.99/yr (FACT, EU-§3.7). Its reviews centre on iCloud sync data loss and on subscription changes that charged for previously free features. Verdict: niche; relevant only as evidence that forced subscription migrations poison store reviews for years.

**Money Pro.** Online banking (Plaid or Salt Edge) only on the $69.99/yr Gold plan, with 12 months of history; the one-time-purchase-to-subscription backlash is its defining review theme (FACT, EU-§3.8). Verdict: not a competitor; a pricing anti-pattern.

**Revolut.** With more than 5 million Italian customers (May 2026), roughly 10% penetration and fifth place among Italian banks by customer count (FACT, medium-high, NEW-6), Revolut is the default secondary account of the S1 segment and therefore the most important ledger Lilleri must read correctly (Italian IBAN, Enable Banking reports of silently skipped booked transactions, G-84). Its analytics are own-ledger (budgets, Subscriptions hub, Pockets; ASSUMPTION on detail); external-account linking for Italian banks is UNKNOWN; it cannot pay F24/MAV/RAV, its support is chat-only, and its September 2026 data-disclosure incident is fresh (FACT, IT-Y-01, IT-W-08). Verdict: a connector priority and a trust-contrast, not a PFM competitor today; watch its linked-accounts and AI-assistant roadmap in Italy (OPEN QUESTION).

**N26.** 8M+ customers EU-wide with Italian onboarding resumed in June 2024 (low reliability); Insights with a fixed taxonomy and Spaces with rules on paid plans (ASSUMPTION), no external aggregation. Verdict: a typical secondary account to reconcile against the primary bank, and a tone-of-voice reference (calm, precise); not a competitor.

**Hype.** A mass-market Italian neobank (minors from 12, cash top-up, cashback, MAV/RAV/PagoPA/F24 payments in-app) with Next €2.90 and Premium €9.90 plans that advertise "aggregazione conti di altre banche" (FACT, low-medium, IT-W-01), and an e-money institution being merged into Banca Sella in 2026 (FACT, low-medium, IT-W-04). Depth of its aggregation is UNKNOWN. Verdict: the second proof (after Intesa) that cross-bank aggregation is table stakes in Italy, a connector whose own data access is UNKNOWN, and a likely source of migration churn; verify its Radar depth before positioning.

**Splitwise.** The global shared-expense default, also in Italian roundups, whose free tier has been capped at roughly four expense adds per day with a 10-second ad between adds since late 2023 (FACT, medium-low, IT-V-13/V-14/W-10) and whose annual Pro price varies by region ($29.99–$59.99). Its API data model (paid/owed shares, repayments, receipts, comments with edit history) defines "good enough" for shared costs (FACT, high, IT-D-01). Verdict: not a PFM competitor; the benchmark Lilleri's household feature must beat by attaching splits to real bank transactions instead of manual entry, and by never capping the free tier.

**Tricount.** bunq-owned since 2022 and now 100% free with Premium deprecated, no ads and no limits; CSV export left with Premium; a bunq card auto-captures expenses; new sign-ups get a bunq account (FACT, medium / low-medium, IT-V-03/V-04/W-12/W-14). Verdict: shows that split-tracking cannot be a paywall lever in Italy (it is free next door) and that a bank funnel is the monetisation a shared-expense tool ends up with.

**Cleo.** A chat-first, persona-driven assistant with ~$150M ARR, an FTC $17M settlement for deceptive cash-advance claims and obstructive cancellation (March 2025), a UK relaunch in February 2026 at £12.99 Pro, and "Autopilot" agents that move money within guardrails (FACT, EU-S-45/S-76, US-S172). Verdict: a tone and agent-UX reference; its lending-led monetisation and regulatory history are the pattern to avoid for a trust-first Italian PFM.

**Finanzguru.** The European business-model benchmark: >3 million users and ~€40M revenue in 2025 on a generous free core (multibanking, categorisation, contract ledger with next debit and notice period, one-tap cancellation) plus a Plus tier (€4.29 monthly / ≈€2.99 annual, trackers disagree) and ~70% of revenue from insurance and switching commissions; investors include Deutsche Bank (~16%) and PayPal Ventures (~8%); own BaFin registration as an account-information service (FACT, EU-S-68/S-69/S-82; finAPI as provider only HYPOTHESIS). Complaints: re-authentication "sometimes monthly" and mis-categorised small merchants. Verdict: Germany-only, but the model Lilleri's business plan should be measured against; its contract ledger is the feature Italian users have no equivalent of.

**Bankin'.** France's mass PFM (free aggregation, categorisation and balance forecast; Plus €4.99/€39.99), owned by Groupe Casino since the 2022 split from Bridge, with reviews of random sync failures, wrong budget maths, fluctuating forecasts and unanswered complaints (FACT, EU-S-72/S-78/S-33a). Verdict: a captive subsidiary, not an independent business; evidence for the "balance forecast is a daily-use feature" claim and for the cost of unresponsive support.

**Fintonic.** Spain's free aggregator pivoted into a credit-scoring and consumer-finance company after an April 2025 restructuring, profitable in September 2025, with reviews of permanently disconnected entities, shrinking coverage, continuous categorisation failures and exports broken since August 2025 (FACT, EU-S-84/S-18). Verdict: the clearest case of a PFM that could not survive as a PFM; a reminder to keep data portability working to the last day.

**Satispay.** Italy's payments champion (6.5M users, 450k+ merchants, >€116M annualised revenue, cards and Plus/Metal/Velvet plans at €3.99/€9.99/€39.99 since July 2026; FACT, medium, IT-W-02/W-03) with a "Budget" refill envelope that creators single out as its most interesting feature (FACT, low-medium). It is an EMI exposing its own PSD2 portal, so its balances are in scope in principle, but no aggregator integration is evidenced (UNKNOWN). Verdict: not a PFM; the most important non-bank balance to model, the Italian subscription-ladder anchor, and a UX reference for a single safe-to-spend envelope.

**Intesa Sanpaolo (XME Banks).** Live since 2020 and free, XME Banks aggregates accounts and cards of other banks, allows transfers from them and, per its launch copy, shows spending categories and an aggregated income/expense view across linked accounts with graphs, filters and labels (FACT, medium, NEW-7, IT-V-09). Per-user learning, transfer pairing across institutions and review flows are not mentioned anywhere (UNKNOWN); usage numbers are UNKNOWN. Verdict: the single most important competitor to verify by installation; it removes "all accounts in one place" as a differentiator and leaves correctness, learning and bank-independence as Lilleri's ground.

**UniCredit / Buddybank.** Buddybank is active in September 2026 (bonuses, Buddy Club 100, MyOne card) and runs its own PSD2 sandbox; a creator rates its categorisation as good when corrected by hand (FACT, low, IT-§2.6). UniCredit multibanking is an ASSUMPTION and its credit-card accounts are not exposed over PSD2 (FACT). Verdict: a connector with a caveat (cards missing) and a mass of S1-type under-35 users; not a PFM competitor until its multibanking depth is verified.

**Poste Italiane (BancoPosta/Postepay).** Thirty million Postepay cards, more than ten million Evolution cards with IBAN and 3.4 billion transactions in 2025 (FACT, medium, NEW-5); a unified app since October 2025 with a wallet and an AI assistant (low-medium); a €12.5M Garante fine over its apps (April 2026). Verdict: not a PFM; the prepaid-as-primary population Lilleri must model ("ricarica Postepay" is a transfer), and a connector whose user-experience quality is UNKNOWN.

**Fast Budget.** The only Italian-born PFM found, with a Salt Edge partnership announced but no evidence that bank sync is live (one low-reliability table calls it manual-entry only at ≈$3/mo; UNKNOWN). Verdict: install and settle it; likely small; its existence does not change the "no Italian aggregator at scale" thesis.

**Oval Money (closed).** The precedent: an Italian-born app that connected many accounts and online banks (not Revolut) in 2019, pivoted into trading after its 2021 acquisition and closed in 2023 with forced account closures (FACT, IT-§2.2). Verdict: the lesson is a durable revenue rail and an un-drifting identity.

**Quicken Simplifi.** The cheapest full PFM in the US ($3.49–$3.99 promo, $6.99 renewal) with a Spending Plan, watchlists, a Refund Tracker and an AI chat that batch-recategorises with approval (FACT, US-§2.5). Verdict: US/CA only; copy the Refund Tracker and the "safe to spend" plan; avoid the renewal step-up.

**Toshl.** Reaches Italy through Salt Edge but only on the Medici plan ($4.99/mo or $3.33/mo yearly) required for every automatic bank connection (FACT, high, EU-S-81). Verdict: proof that Italian sync is a paid feature in every generic tracker; otherwise marginal.

**Outbank, Linxo, Dyme.** Rules-based German banking app (€3.99/€39.99, can initiate payments); Crédit Agricole's captive French PFM (Premium €4.49/€29.99, 30-day balance forecast); Dutch subscription-cancellation and switching app on Tink (Silver €4.99 / Gold €9.99). Verdict: none serve Italy; together they fix the European Plus anchor and show which narrow features (contracts, forecast, cancellation) earn loyalty.

**bunq.** Twenty-five sub-accounts with their own IBANs, user-defined categories at API level, Tricount as a funnel, and reviews about weeks-long "Safety Mode" freezes and AI-only support (FACT, low / high for the API). Verdict: a true-envelope reference and a small Italian connector; not a competitor.

**ChatGPT personal finance.** A US-only bundle (Plaid, 12,000+ institutions) for Plus/Pro users with balances, spending, subscriptions, upcoming payments, net worth and Q&A (FACT, US-§3). Verdict: the platform-bundling threat to watch; Italians already approximate it manually with exported statements, which is evidence of demand for the output, not for a chat box.

**Actual Budget (open source).** Not a competitor but the richest readable evidence base: automatic transfer recognition (382 votes), merge unmatched (121), periodic sync (118), consent expiry as a 400 error, silent drops and overwrites from Enable Banking, future-dated items inflating balances (FACT, high, PP-§A). Verdict: the specification of what "reconciliation done right" must handle, written by the users who tried hardest to do it by hand.

## 7. Decisions / Recommendations

1. **DECISION (proposed): benchmark set.** Copilot = categorisation learning and one-tap review; Monarch = rules-driven review status and household; YNAB = import matching, trial and API; Wallet = transfer data model (to be extended with automatic cross-institution pairing); Finanzguru = free core + contract ledger + commission rail; Simplifi = Refund Tracker and safe-to-spend plan; Satispay = single envelope UX; Splitwise API = shared-cost data model. Re-implement originally; do not copy.
2. **DECISION (proposed): the competitive claim is "correct across institutions, learns you, nothing to do"**, not "all accounts in one place" (Intesa and Hype already sell that) and not "AI budgeting" (noise).
3. **DECISION (proposed): never ship the documented anti-patterns** — "clear cache" or "check your bank" as a fix; hidden pending that reappears; manual-only transfer tagging; one rule per payee; 30-category caps; gated custom categories/rules/export; 7-day trials; silent renewal step-ups; lifetime deals that vanish; web-only export; lending or data-selling rails; mascots and hype vocabulary.
4. **RECOMMENDATION: verify by installation before Phase 2** — Intesa XME Banks, Hype Next (paid), UniCredit app, Revolut linked accounts (IT), Fast Budget, Wallet on an Italian bank set. Record categorisation of external transactions, transfer pairing, learning, and the consent UX of each.
5. **RECOMMENDATION: keep this matrix alive.** Prices and free tiers moved materially within 2025–26 (Snoop +20%, Spendee ×2, Finanzguru +43% monthly, PocketGuard free tier removed, Monarch Plus added); re-check the Block A commercial columns quarterly from the vendor pages.
