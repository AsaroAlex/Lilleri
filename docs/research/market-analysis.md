## 1. Executive summary (decision-relevant)

1. **No Italian product is documented to reconcile money across institutions — but "no Italian aggregator exists" is wrong.** Independent Italian aggregators exist and are small or have retreated: Plannix (planning/wealth on Salt Edge + GoCardless rails, ">35,000 members", advisory-style paid tiers), EasyPol (PagoPA payments plus an aggregator with automatic and custom categorisation, ">500,000 users" claimed), Fast Budget (Salt Edge partnership announced, live sync UNKNOWN) and Switcho (>1M users, which **removed bank-account connections on 15 Aug 2025** and now sells utility/telco switching through bank partners). Oval Money closed in 2023. The apps Italian finance creators recommend remain foreign Salt Edge trackers (Wallet, Spendee, MoneyWiz, Money Pro, Toshl) whose Italian reviews are dominated by linking failures. FACT (medium, company copy and trade press seen as snippets: NEW-14, NEW-15, NEW-16, `user-pain-points.md` WS-15/WS-16; IT-§1, EU-§7, PP-G I-2); HYPOTHESIS that none of them pairs transfers or learns from corrections (absence in copy, products not installed).
2. **The underlying population is large; how multi-banked it is, is not measured.** 48.1 million current accounts at end-2024 incl. firms (FACT, NEW-1); more than 67 million payment cards at end-2023 (FACT, NEW-2); "30 million Postepay cards" is Poste's company copy and may be cumulative issuance rather than cards in use, so it is not a population figure (FACT medium for the claim; NEW-5); Revolut above 5 million Italian customers in May 2026 (FACT, NEW-6); Satispay 6.5 million users (FACT low, company copy via a GitHub capture, IT-W-02/W-03). That the target user holds **3–5 institutions** rests on two creator transcripts (HYPOTHESIS, IT-Y-02/Y-03); the share of Italian adults with ≥2 institutions is **UNKNOWN** (no public Banca d'Italia figure surfaced, NEW-21; route: IBF microdata, IACOFI).
3. **"See all your accounts in one place" — and categorising them — is table stakes in Italy.** Intesa Sanpaolo XME Banks (2020, free) aggregates other banks' accounts and cards and its "Gestione Spese" categorises movements of all linked accounts with editable categories, labels and splits (FACT medium, NEW-7, WS-04); Hype Radar on Next/Premium does "analisi e categorizzazione delle uscite e delle entrate su tutti i conti collegati" (FACT, official help centre, WS-01); Banco BPM YouConnect and Webank WeConnect link other banks' accounts and cards and auto-categorise movements (FACT medium, NEW-17); Revolut **launched** linked external accounts for Italian banks in August 2020 via TrueLayer — current availability UNKNOWN (NEW-18, WS-06/WS-14). What none of them *documents* is cross-institution transfer and card-settlement pairing, learning from corrections, or a review flow — UNKNOWN, not proven absent; B2B vendors (Tink, Salt Edge, CRIF, Fabrick) sell categorisation and transfer flags to any of them (`open-banking-providers.md` §3, §4.2).
4. **The same failure modes are documented in every market:** connections break and need re-authentication (N=22 independent sources), duplicates (16), consent expiry experienced as random breakage (11), transfers and card settlements not recognised (10; 382 up-votes on the most-voted Actual Budget feature request), categorisation that "never learns" (10). FACT that these failure modes exist (PP-§A, US-§4.1); the N counts are dominated by self-hosters wiring GoCardless/Enable Banking themselves, so **Italian mass-market frequency is UNKNOWN** until the review coding and interviews in §9.
5. **The market re-formed around paid subscriptions after Mint died (March 2024)** — Monarch reached $100M ARR and 1M members by 2026 with no free tier, YNAB holds $109/yr, Copilot $95/yr — while in Europe subscription-only D2C apps without a second rail or a parent exited (Moneyhub, Spiir, Yolt, Grip, Oval Money). Survivors: Finanzguru (>3M users, ~€40M 2025 revenue; ~70% of **2022** revenue from insurance/contract commissions), Emma (profitable on £5.7M 2024 revenue "thanks to a paywall" — 2 free bank logins), Plum (profitable on AUM). FACT (US-§1, US-W12, EU-§1, EU-§5, NEW-20). Revenue per user is low: Emma ≈ £0.16 (≈ €0.19) per claimed user per month; Finanzguru ≈ €1.1 (HYPOTHESIS, arithmetic in §3.3).
6. **The EU "Plus" price band moved up in 2025–26 to €3.99–€5.99 monthly / €30–€48 annual**, and Italians already pay €2.90–€9.99 a month for neobank and payment-app tiers (Hype, Satispay, N26, Revolut, Plum IT). FACT (EU-§8, IT-§2.3/2.4, PP-§F). Willingness to pay for a *PFM* in Italy is UNKNOWN.
7. **AI moved from "chat over your data" (2023–25) to "agents that act" (2026: Rocket Money Rowan, Cleo Autopilot, Piere) and to platform bundling (ChatGPT personal finance with Plaid, US-only).** Per-user learning categorisation (Copilot) remains the capability users praise most; no vendor publishes a verifiable accuracy number. In Italy the only "AI budgeting" users visibly practise is pasting a Revolut export into a temporary ChatGPT chat. FACT (US-§3, IT-§5.4); reading ASSUMPTION.
8. **Open-banking plumbing in Italy is mature but uneven:** CBI Globe processed more than 700 million API calls for 200+ third-party providers (FACT, NEW-8); CRIF reports that 57.4% of account-connection attempts succeeded in H1 2025 **inside its own account-aggregation services** (≈286k counterparties, credit-assessment flows; FACT medium, NEW-4/NEW-19) — a demand/trust signal, not a connector-quality baseline for a PFM; GoCardless Bank Account Data is closed to new accounts since July 2025 (FACT, PP-AD-01); Intesa Sanpaolo rate-limits unattended access and serves transactions in two-week windows (FACT, PP-§G I-5); UniCredit, **Banca Mediolanum** and Crédit Agricole Italia card accounts are not exposed **through Enable Banking** as of March 2026 (FACT for that provider, PP-D-OB-#63; other providers UNKNOWN).
9. **Why Lilleri should exist — and it is not "because it uses AI":** the job "know what actually happens to my money across several institutions without doing the bookkeeping myself, and trust the numbers" is real abroad, measurable in minutes per month, and **not documented as solved** in Italy by banks (aggregation and categorisation exist; cross-institution reconciliation is not documented), by foreign trackers (sync breaks, no documented learning or reconciliation) or by ChatGPT (export, paste, privacy workaround). The founding hypothesis "le tue finanze si organizzano da sole" is **consistent with the pain-point evidence but untested with users**; the evidence suggests one amendment — automation is trusted only when it is visible and correctable — so the promise to test is "si organizzano da sole — e tu vedi sempre perché". HYPOTHESIS.
10. **Gate A verdict: NOT YET ASSESSABLE** (revised from "PASS WITH CONDITIONS"; §9). Problem existence and population order of magnitude are supported; whether the problem is *unserved in Italy*, whether the economics close and whether the licence route exists are not. A dated checklist (incumbent depth test, Enable Banking pilot, counsel answer on route A, RFP quotes, 500-review coding plus 20–30 interviews, creator CAC pilot) must close before a verdict. Zero-regret Phase 1 work proceeds; connector-layer identity design, pairing-engine parameters and any multi-aggregator consent UX are frozen until the pilot and counsel report.
11. **An uncapped free tier does not close.** With three billable accounts per free user, covering variable costs alone needs **15% / 36% / 68%** of connected users to pay at €0.10 / €0.30 / €0.60 per account-month (HYPOTHESIS, §3.3); no freemium PFM evidences such conversion (UNKNOWN for every competitor; the only cross-category benchmark is RevenueCat 2026 as summarised in `business-model.md` §1.6: median ≈ 2.1% for freemium apps vs ≈ 10.7% for hard-paywall apps by day 35, medium). The earlier "generous free core with all connections" posture is **withdrawn**; this document adopts `business-model.md` D-BM-1 (free = one institution, ≤ 2 synced accounts, everything that makes data correct always free; 30-day trial; guardrail and fallback) with the trial-gated model as the pre-agreed comparator. Even so, the Base case does not break even at any scale (`unit-economics.md` §9): the economics are a Gate A condition, not a settled fact.

### 3.3 Unit economics of the free tier — why the free core must be capped (HYPOTHESIS; arithmetic shown)

The first draft of this document proposed a "generous free core" with all connections free. No document multiplied that by the bank-data cost. The table does it with the Base inputs of `unit-economics.md` (owner of the model): Plus net revenue €2.85 per subscription-month (€4.99 / €39.99, 60% annual, 22% VAT, 13% blended take); other variable COGS €0.110 per connected free user and €0.166 per paying member (AI, infra, notifications, support, other); a paying member connects 3.0 billable accounts; an unconnected free user costs €0.077. ## 8. Why should Lilleri exist?

### 8.1 The job to be done (not the feature list)

"**Know what actually happens to my money across all my accounts, without doing the bookkeeping myself, and trust the numbers.**" The evidence for each clause:

- *across all my accounts*: S1 behaviour as described by creators (IT-Y-02/Y-03; FACT low-medium), 48.1M accounts, >5M Revolut (NEW-1, NEW-6) — FACT for the counts; how many Italians hold ≥2 institutions is UNKNOWN (§3.1).
- *without doing the bookkeeping*: the effort ladder in minutes (IT-Y-02), YNAB's "manual effort" as a churn reason, Actual's 382-vote transfer request, the "merge/match" request with 121 votes (PP-§A #3, #16, PP-§D) — FACT that the need is expressed (mostly by non-Italian and technical users); Italian frequency UNKNOWN.
- *trust the numbers*: "cannot trust the numbers" is the top churn reason (PP-§D); Trustpilot/BBB scores of 2.0–3.5 versus 4.5–4.9 in app stores for the same products, driven by billing and support rather than features (US-§4.5) — FACT.

The task brief explicitly forbids "because it uses AI" as the answer, and the evidence agrees: no user in any source asks for AI; they ask for correctness and for less work. "AI" appears in the evidence only as (a) the mechanism Copilot uses to learn per user, (b) marketing noise from SEO-driven apps, and (c) a workaround Italians practise with ChatGPT because nothing native exists (US-§3, IT-Y-02). AI is a means for categorisation learning and explanation; it is not the reason to exist.

**Verdict (HYPOTHESIS — consistent with the pain-point evidence, untested with users; the 20–30 S1/S2 interviews are a Gate A precondition, §9):** the evidence supports the hypothesis with an amendment; no Italian user has yet been asked. "You don't have to manage it" is the right promise; "you never see what it did" is not. The promise should be stated as **"le tue finanze si organizzano da sole — e tu vedi sempre perché"** ("your finances organise themselves — and you can always see why"). The hero moment is an empty Review Inbox the user *looks at* ("niente da fare"), consistent with the brand territory in BR-§8.3.

Draft positioning sentence (DECISION proposed, to be tested): *Collega i tuoi conti una volta. Lilleri riconosce da sola bonifici interni, addebiti della carta, rimborsi e abbonamenti, impara le tue categorie e ti mostra solo ciò che vale la pena controllare.*

## 10. Decisions / Recommendations

Each item is a proposed DECISION to be recorded in an ADR or in `product/`; none is final until recorded.

1. **DECISION (proposed): position Lilleri on correctness and effort, not on AI.** Headline promise "le tue finanze si organizzano da sole — e tu vedi sempre perché"; "AI" appears only as the explanation of how categorisation learns. Rationale: §8.1–8.2.
2. **DECISION (proposed): launch segment = S1 multi-account optimisers and S2 broken-sync refugees**, reached through Italian finance creators; S3 households as the first expansion. Rationale: §4.
3. **DECISION (proposed): "see all accounts in one place" is table stakes; the differentiators are automatic reconciliation (transfers, card settlements, refunds, pending→posted, duplicates), per-user learning with explicit rules winning, the Review Inbox, proactive consent management and Italian payment semantics.** Rationale: §6, §7, T6, T7.
4. **DECISION (proposed): pricing posture = generous free core + Plus at €3.99–€4.99 monthly / €29.99–€39.99 annual; never gate custom categories, rules or export; reminder before any trial converts; no ads in the product.** Rationale: T5, T9, PP-§F, EU-§11–12.
5. **DECISION (proposed): plan a second revenue rail before scale** (B2B2C with an Italian bank or aligned, disclosed affiliate), explicitly excluding lending/cash-advance and data selling. Rationale: T4, EU-§9, PP-§E.
6. **DECISION (proposed): provider-agnostic connector layer with a per-bank health page in Italian; honest coverage per account type (credit cards of UniCredit/Mediolanum/Crédit Agricole are not on PSD2); CSV/PDF import as a first-class fallback; GoCardless excluded.** Rationale: T3, PP-§G.
7. **DECISION (proposed): trust features ship in the MVP:** consent screen naming the licensed AISP with read-only wording in Italian, "what we store and who we send it to" page, CSV export and account deletion from day one. Rationale: T8, PP-§E.
8. **RECOMMENDATION: treat Finanzguru (free core + commission rail, >3M users) as the business-model benchmark, Copilot (per-user learning, one-tap review) as the categorisation benchmark, Monarch (rules-driven review status, household) as the review-flow benchmark, YNAB (34-day trial, API, import IDs) as the trust-and-portability benchmark.**
9. **RECOMMENDATION: no money-movement automation in the MVP** (no round-ups, no auto-saves, no agents); read-only bookkeeping until trust is established. Rationale: §8.2 (Revolut "Spiccioli" reaction, Cleo/Rocket regulatory history).
10. **RECOMMENDATION: re-verify every number in §3 on the primary page before it enters the business plan**; the FABI, Banca d'Italia, ECB SPACE, CRIF and Revolut figures above are search-engine summaries.
