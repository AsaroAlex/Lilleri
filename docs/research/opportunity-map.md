## 1. Summary

1. **The wedge is reconciliation correctness, not feature breadth.** The five failure modes with the broadest recorded support (connections break, duplicates, unpaired transfers and card settlements, consent expiry as random breakage, categorisation that never learns) are exactly documented failure areas in the retained sample (FACT of reports; Italian prevalence and competitor depth UNKNOWN, PP-§A, US-§4.1). Areas 3–10 below are the MVP core.
2. **Automation must be visible.** Silent automation is a documented trust-killer (hidden pending that reappears, invented transfer counterparts, silently skipped transactions); the pattern that users praise is "automatic, with one tap to confirm and an obvious undo" (FACT, US-S29, PP-G-24/G-83/G-84, US-S51). Every improvement below therefore pairs an automatic action with a reason and an undo.
3. **Italian semantics are a candidate product asset; competitor holdings are UNKNOWN**: F24, MAV/RAV, PagoPA/CBILL, bollettini, "ricarica Postepay", prepaid-as-primary, cash top-ups, bimonthly utilities, semiannual insurance, TARI (FACT for the types; HYPOTHESIS for the gap, IT-§8).
4. **Twenty differentiators are ranked in §3 by user value × feasibility × defensibility**; the top five are per-user learning with explicit rules winning and a visible "why", automatic transfer and card-settlement pairing, Italian payment-type and merchant semantics, the Review Inbox, and the pending→posted/duplicate state machine. Consent management and sync transparency follow and cost little.
5. **The MVP deliberately does not build** money movement, agents, bill negotiation, lending, investments analytics, receipt OCR, a chat assistant, affiliate offers, gamification, manual split groups or a lifetime deal (§4). Each exclusion is tied to evidence that it either harms trust, is already free next door, or is a feature-count trap.

## 2. Feature-area opportunities

### 2.1 Onboarding (zero-setup, value before configuration)

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Copilot (first value in one session: connect, confirm detected recurring items, review; ML switches on after ~30 reviews, FACT high, US-S61/S51); Origin (AI builds a budget "in seconds", swipe review, FACT high, US-S141/S143); YNAB's 34-day trial without a card (FACT, US-S13); Simplifi's Spending Plan auto-built from detected bills (FACT, US-S101).
- **USER PROBLEM:** "Where did my money go" at month-end is the hook, and spreadsheets and "1000 apps" get abandoned (FACT low-medium, IT-Y-02); Monarch's 7-day trial is "too short to finish setup" and YNAB takes 2–4 weeks to feel comfortable (FACT, US-S39/S13); Spendee's Italian users waited three weeks to connect a bank (FACT low-medium, EU-S-13a).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Sign up → connect → customise categories → set a budget (Monarch, 4 steps); YNAB asks the user to adopt a method before showing value; trackers show an empty ledger until sync works.
- **WEAKNESS:** Value is gated behind configuration (categories, budgets, rules) and behind a sync that may take days; short trials expire before the first full month of data; card-up-front trials convert into billing anger (FACT, PP-§A #7, #23).
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** One screen, one consent, then a first "Cosa è successo ai tuoi soldi" view built entirely from what the bank returned: income, fixed costs detected, transfers already paired, subscriptions found, safe-to-spend. No category setup, no budget method, no card. Proposed 30-day non-renewing Plus preview without card, following `business-model.md`; the free closed beta has no paid trial, with a reminder before any conversion. Steps-to-value target: 3 (install, consent at the bank, read the summary). HYPOTHESIS to test: a 90-day history pull is enough to detect recurring items on day one for most S1 users (depends on provider history depth, PP-§A #18).
- *Copy:* "Collega una volta. Poi niente da fare."

### 2.2 Connection UX and coverage honesty

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Monarch's provider choice per institution with user override and manual fallback (FACT high, US-S21/S22); Plum's three aggregators for resilience (FACT, EU-S-28); YNAB's +21% UK / +12% EU conversion after switching European provider (FACT high, vendor metric, US-W3); Enable Banking's Italian market page listing Intesa, UniCredit, Banco BPM, BCC Iccrea, Crédit Agricole Italia, BPER, MPS, Mediolanum, Poste (FACT, PP-D-OB-#63).
- **USER PROBLEM:** The number-one complaint in every market: connections break, re-auth loops, "cannot link or change bank" (22 retained source entries, FACT, PP-§A #1); Italian specifics: Mediobanca Premier blank page, BCC Banca d'Alba lists no accounts, Intesa via GoCardless errors after login, ING Italy and Credem return 422 on transactions (FACT high, PP-G-81/G-82/G-86/G-14); card accounts of UniCredit, Mediolanum and Crédit Agricole Italia were absent through Enable Banking in March2026 (FACT provider observation; other/current coverage UNKNOWN, PP-§G I-8); CRIF reports57.4% connection completion in its H1-2025 credit-assessment cohort (FACT source observation, NEW-4); this is not a PFM connector failure baseline.
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** One aggregator (Salt Edge for the trackers; Plaid for the US apps); a generic bank list; "sync failed" states; advertised banks that no longer connect (Spendee IT).
- **WEAKNESS:** Coverage is promised at the bank level, not at the account-type level; failures are silent and un-attributed; no fallback besides "try again"; GoCardless, the free route, is closed to new accounts since July 2025 (FACT, PP-AD-01).
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** A provider-agnostic connector layer with a mock-first adapter boundary; a future second provider requires its own consent/terms and conservative identifier reconciliation, not silent consent migration, and a live, Italian-language coverage and health page per bank and per account type (conto, carta di credito, prepagata, deposito). Where an account type is not reachable (UniCredit credit cards), say so before the user tries, and offer validated CSV import of that statement, with PDF parsing Later as a first-class path. Connection flow names the licensed AISP and says "solo lettura" in Italian (FACT that creators must reassure viewers on this, IT-Y-02). Measure bank authorisation completion and first complete data load separately with attempted-user denominators. CRIF’s credit-flow 57.4% sample is contextual, not a comparable PFM benchmark (Gate A condition 1). Trade-off noted: two aggregators cost more (cost ranks 11th of 13 priorities; reliability ranks 6th).
- *Copy:* "Lilleri legge i movimenti tramite [AISP], autorizzato a operare in Italia. Solo lettura: nessuno può muovere denaro dal tuo conto."

### 2.3 Consent renewal (90/180 days)

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Renewal experience is a research opportunity (HYPOTHESIS); absence in marketing is not verified absence (EU-§6); the regulation itself: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/2360 makes the 180-day SCA exemption mandatory for banks since 25 Jul 2023 (FACT high, EU-S-87); Salt Edge exposes `period_days` / `expires_at` per consent and `max_consent_days` per provider (FACT high, EU-S-55); Emma's "timer restarts on renewal" wording (FACT, EU-S-21).
- **USER PROBLEM:** Consent expiry surfaces as unexplained breakage: a 400 "EUA was valid for 90 days and it expired", a 500 page, "sync offline", re-auth "sometimes monthly after updates" (FACT, PP-§A #4, 11 sources); help pages still say 90 days (Emma, Finanzguru), aggregator EULAs say "usually up to 90", BudgetBakers says "1–6 months depending on your bank" (FACT, EU-§6).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Nothing until it breaks; then a generic "reconnect your bank".
- **WEAKNESS:** The user cannot plan, batches of renewals hit at random, each renewal is an SCA round-trip, and the reason is never explained; Italian per-bank consent windows are UNKNOWN and inconsistent (PP-§G I-4).
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** Consent is a first-class object with a per-connection expiry countdown, a pre-expiry push seven and two days before, one-tap batch renewal of everything expiring in the same fortnight, and a plain-language explanation ("la legge europea chiede una nuova conferma alla banca ogni 180 giorni; alcune banche ogni 90"). Store `expires_at` from the provider, never assume 90. Record per-bank actual windows to feed the health page.
- *Copy:* "L'accesso a Intesa Sanpaolo scade tra 12 giorni. Rinnova ora: un minuto, nessun dato da reinserire."

### 2.4 Sync health and transparency (every failure named)

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** GoCardless rate-limit headers (`account_success_limit`, `remaining`, `reset`) and per-endpoint limits (FACT, PP-GC-01); Yapily's published data restrictions for Intesa Sanpaolo (HTTP 429 `ACCESS_EXCEEDED`, two-week transaction window; FACT, PP-D-OBA-#195); Plaid's `/transactions/refresh` and `TRANSACTIONS_REMOVED` webhook (FACT, US-V4); the Actual Budget request "it would help a lot if skipped transactions were logged or surfaced in the import summary" (FACT, PP-G-84).
- **USER PROBLEM:** Opaque failures ("sync offline", "internal error", blank pages, "contact support") are a documented trust-killer (FACT, PP-§E, G-73/G-60); silent drops, overwrites and merges leave balances short "with no error, no warning" (FACT high, PP-G-83/G-84/G-87); the 4-unattended-refreshes-per-day cap means five accounts cannot all be synced in one day on some providers (FACT, PP-G-07); Monarch tells users to "allow 24 hours" (FACT, US-S25).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Generic error states; "clear the cache" (Copilot), "re-sync" with a one-week cooldown (Empower), "check your bank" (Monarch).
- **WEAKNESS:** The user cannot tell whether the bank, the provider, the consent or the app failed, or when it will be retried; user-present access differs under the RTS; bank/provider operational limits and app behaviour require testing (FACT/UNKNOWN, PP-§G I-5).
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** Every sync state names the bank, the cause (consent expired, bank maintenance, provider error, daily cap reached, two-week window chunking in progress) and the next retry time; refresh-on-open as a user-present request; a per-sync summary that lists items added, paired, skipped or changed, with a link to each; an import ledger that never overwrites an existing transaction silently (positional reference IDs are treated as unstable, PP-G-83). Reliability is priority 6; this is where it is earned.
- *Copy:* "Intesa Sanpaolo: limite giornaliero di aggiornamenti raggiunto. Riprovo automaticamente alle 18:00. Puoi aggiornare ora aprendo l'app."

### 2.5 Review Inbox ("inbox zero for your finances")

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Monarch's "needs review / reviewed" status set by rules on amount, merchant, category or account, assignable to a household member, with splitting counting as review (FACT high, US-S28/S26); Copilot's "To Review" where each confirmation trains the model and the top-2 guesses make correction one tap (FACT high, US-S54/S51); Origin's swipe review (FACT, US-S143); YNAB's approve-and-match flow with a 10-day manual-vs-import window (FACT high, US-S2); the Actual Budget requests to approve/edit/reject synced items and to see which were just imported (FACT, PP-G-04/G-45).
- **USER PROBLEM:** Users want to validate what the sync did without re-reading the whole ledger; "no graceful way to merge" (121 votes), "identify recently imported" (FACT, PP-G-43/G-45); "too much manual work (approving, transfers, credit cards)" is a churn reason (FACT, PP-§D); Rocket, Simplifi, Empower and PocketGuard have no inbox at all (FACT, US-§4.3).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Either everything needs approval (YNAB) or nothing does (Rocket), or rules decide (Monarch) without a confidence signal.
- **WEAKNESS:** Approval-everything is work; approve-nothing is silent; rule-only routing needs set-up effort (a Monarch complaint, FACT, US-S39); nobody shows an empty state as the goal.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** The Inbox receives only what is uncertain or user-defined: low-confidence categorisations, unpaired candidate transfers, new merchants, amounts above a user threshold, items a household partner must see. Everything else is auto-reviewed with a reason attached. Each card is a one-tap confirm with the top alternatives inline, swipe on mobile, and an undo stack. The empty state is the hero moment ("Niente da fare") and is reached by design, not by abandonment (HYPOTHESIS from BR-§8.3 verbal territory). Household assignment arrives with the household feature (2.15).
- *Copy:* "Niente da fare. Tutto è al suo posto." · "3 cose da controllare: un nuovo negozio, un possibile giroconto, una spesa sopra 200 €."

### 2.6 Categorisation learning (personal, explicit rules win)

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Copilot Intelligence: one ML model per user trained on that user's reviewed transactions, predicting type and category, top-2 shown, active after ~30 reviews (FACT high, US-S51); Emma's official "learns after three similar edits" (FACT, EU-S-21); YNAB's payee memory (FACT, US-S5); the Italian creator who writes rules for an unmapped local supermarket (FACT low-medium, IT-Y-02); the ChatGPT-DIY workflow whose output users actually want (FACT low-medium, IT-Y-02).
- **USER PROBLEM:** "Categorisation wrong and does not learn" (10 sources, FACT, PP-§A #5): Snoop's sums "bore no relation to reality", Emma "doesn't seem to ever learn", Finanzguru wrong for small merchants, Fintonic "continuous failures"; corrections are paywalled (Emma Pro, Rocket Premium, Snoop Plus, Linxo Premium) or capped (Empower 30 categories, Simplifi one rule per payee) (FACT, PP-§A #15).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Provider category plus a fixed taxonomy (most), payee memory (YNAB), silent threshold learning (Emma), per-user ML without explanation (Copilot), "95% accuracy" claims from third parties (ASSUMPTION low, US-S65).
- **WEAKNESS:** Learning is invisible, so users do not believe it; explicit rules and learned behaviour fight each other; nobody explains why a category was chosen; cold start is opaque.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** A per-user model trained only on that user's confirmations, layered under explicit rules that always win; the first correction of a merchant proposes a rule ("Da ora CONAD CITY → Spesa alimentare?") instead of waiting for three; every auto-category carries a reason ("imparato dalla tua correzione del 12 marzo"; "stesso negozio di 14 movimenti precedenti"; "tipo di pagamento: F24 → Tasse"); a visible "readiness" indicator during cold start; confidence shown as words, never as a percentage. Learned rules live in a reviewable list next to explicit ones. Base taxonomy localised to Italy with fixed system buckets (Transfer, Unknown income/expense, Uncategorised) as in Wallet's model (FACT, IT-D-03). This is the only place "AI" appears in the product, and it appears as an explanation.
- *Copy:* "Spesa alimentare — imparato da te il 12 marzo. Non è giusto? Correggi con un tocco, Lilleri lo ricorda."

### 2.7 Rules

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Monarch's if-then rules on merchant, amount, account and category with actions rename, set category, add tags, set review status, assign reviewer, split by % or €, hide, apply retroactively (FACT high, US-S27); Outbank's two systems (rules-driven categories plus manual tags) (FACT high, EU-S-31); Simplifi's "apply to similar" memory (FACT high, US-S95).
- **USER PROBLEM:** Rules are needed for local merchants and Italian payment types but are paywalled or limited; rule set-up effort is itself a complaint (FACT, US-S39, PP-§A #15).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** A rules screen the user must discover and populate; one rule per payee (Simplifi); rules only on paid tiers (Emma Pro, Rocket Premium, PocketGuard Plus).
- **WEAKNESS:** Rules are a power-user chore disconnected from the moment of correction; precedence between rules and learning is undefined; retroactive application is rare.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** Rules are created from corrections (proposed inline, one tap), never from a blank form first; multiple rules per merchant allowed; conditions on merchant, amount range, account, payment type (F24/MAV/RAV/SDD/bollettino), direction; actions: category, rename, tag, mark transfer, mark reimbursable, split, route to Inbox, assign to partner; retroactive by default with a preview count; rules win over the learned model, and the model learns from rules. Rules are free on every tier (gating corrections blocks the learning that makes categorisation good, FACT, EU-§12 #2).

### 2.8 Merchant normalisation and Italian payment-type semantics

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Copilot and Monarch's cleaned names with logos (FACT medium/ASSUMPTION, US-S63/S43); Hype listing MAV/RAV, PagoPA/CBILL and simplified F24 as in-app functions (FACT low-medium, IT-W-01); bunq exposing MCC and counterparty alias on payments (FACT high, IT-D-02); the Actual Budget complaint that rules strip card numbers and dates the user sometimes needs, and that payee IBANs are missing (FACT, PP-G-53/G-57).
- **USER PROBLEM:** Cryptic merchant strings, payee lists that explode after a holiday, lost detail after cleanup (FACT, PP-§A #19); global categorisers treat F24, MAV/RAV, bollettini, PagoPA and "ricarica Postepay" as "Other" or transfer noise (HYPOTHESIS, IT-§8.1); Revolut cannot even pay them (FACT low-medium, IT-Y-01).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Generic merchant dictionaries built for US/UK; logo APIs; rename rules.
- **WEAKNESS:** No Italian merchant dictionary (Conad, Esselunga, Pam, local BCC descriptors, SDD mandates of utilities), no payment-type parsing, and cleanup that destroys the raw string.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** Keep the raw descriptor always visible one tap away; normalise to a merchant entity with logo and, for Italian payment types, a typed semantic (F24 → taxes/INPS; MAV/RAV → fees, fines, condominium; bollettino/PagoPA → utilities and public services; CBILL; SDD/RID → recurring direct debit; "ricarica Postepay"/Satispay top-up → transfer to a wallet account, not a spend); store counterparty IBAN where provided to answer "have I already paid this account". The Italian merchant dictionary is a data asset that grows from user confirmations (defensibility, §3).
- *Copy:* "F24 — Agenzia delle Entrate: Tasse e contributi" · "Ricarica Postepay: giroconto verso la tua Postepay Evolution, non una spesa."

### 2.9 Transfers and card settlements (pairing across institutions)

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Wallet's transfer model: locked auto-managed Transfer category excluded from spend, mirror record with opposite sign and a shared transfer id, states paired/unpaired/orphan/re-bound/cleared, exact opposite amounts for same-currency pairs, FX-tolerant cross-currency pairs (FACT high, IT-D-03); Copilot's typed transactions with both legs of a card payment as Internal Transfer (FACT high, US-S52/S53); YNAB's credit-card-payment-as-transfer with auto-funded category (FACT high, US-S5); Simplifi marking the counterpart pending when one side is split (FACT high, US-S92); Monarch's pairing (UNKNOWN; help article blocked, US-S30).
- **USER PROBLEM:** The single most up-voted request in the largest open-source PFM: recognise transfers between accounts automatically; fixing by hand "usually creates a mess of duplicates" (382 votes, FACT high, PP-G-01); follow-ups ask for an "is this the same transaction?" prompt and a bulk "potential transfers" screen (FACT, PP-G-02/G-03); credit-card payments counted twice or mis-modelled; statement vs current balance confusion; card payment amounts vary so schedules fail (FACT, PP-§A #17); a reimbursement shows up as income you never had (FACT, PP-G-04).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Manual "Internal Transfer" category (Rocket, Empower); explicit transfer transactions the user must match (YNAB); typed transfers within one aggregator's view (Copilot); auto-managed Transfer category without evidenced cross-institution auto-pairing (Wallet).
- **WEAKNESS:** Pairing is either manual or confined to one ledger; timing differences (a bonifico leaves on Friday and arrives on Monday), fees, FX and card-statement cycles are not handled; the app sometimes invents a counterpart (FACT, PP-G-24).
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** An automatic cross-institution pairing engine with an explicit state machine: candidate pairs are proposed from opposite amounts within a date window (bonifico 0–3 business days, instant 0, card settlement by statement cycle), same owner, counterparty IBAN or card reference when available; high-confidence pairs are paired automatically with a reason and an undo; medium-confidence pairs go to the Inbox as one card ("è lo stesso movimento?"); a pair is never created by inventing a leg; card settlements (saldo carta di credito, Postepay top-up, Satispay top-up, Revolut top-up) are first-class pair types so spend is counted once at purchase. Transfers are excluded from income and expense by default and shown in a separate "Giroconti" view. Net worth changes only when money leaves the household. This is Lilleri's defining capability (§3 D1/D2).
- *Copy:* "Bonifico 500 € da Fineco a Revolut: riconosciuto come giroconto. Non conta come spesa né come entrata. Annulla"

### 2.10 Duplicates and pending → posted

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Plaid's model: posted record carries `pending_transaction_id`, the pending one is removed, `TRANSACTIONS_REMOVED` webhook, unlinked only "in rare instances" (FACT high, US-V4); YNAB's deterministic import IDs (amount + date + occurrence index), identical-row skip on file import, 10-day manual-vs-import matching, `duplicate_import_ids` in its API (FACT high, US-S2/S3/V1); YNAB's policy of keeping pending out of the plan until cleared (FACT high, US-S4).
- **USER PROBLEM:** Duplicates after re-link, pending→posted, PayPal plus bank, transfer legs, provider glitches (16 sources, FACT, PP-§A #2); "deleting brings them back"; Enable Banking creating every transaction twice when the bank returns no IDs, overwriting an income row with a purchase when positional IDs shift, silently skipping a booked Revolut transaction (FACT high, PP-G-22/G-83/G-84); dedup that drops legitimate identical transactions (FACT, PP-G-87); future-dated items inflating balances (70 comments, FACT, PP-G-05/G-36); pending posting on the wrong date with notes from unrelated transactions (FACT medium, US-S62).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** "Clear local cache" (Copilot), "check the bank, check double-linked accounts, else delete" (Monarch), "ignore" (Rocket), editing a pending item before it posts creates a duplicate (Simplifi).
- **WEAKNESS:** No explicit state machine; aggregator linkage is discarded; deletion is not remembered; fuzzy matching both over- and under-matches; balances disagree with the bank.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** A transaction lifecycle with explicit states (pending, booked, future-dated/pre-notified, removed-by-bank, user-deleted) keyed on provider IDs first, then stable composite IDs, never positional ones; pending→booked replaces in place and preserves user edits; "balance as the bank shows it today" as the default view with future-dated items shown separately; a remembered-deletion list so nothing respawns; identical same-day items collapsed only with the same provider ID, otherwise kept and flagged; cross-source duplicates (PayPal + bank, Satispay + bank) paired as settlements, not deleted. Every merge is visible in the sync summary (2.4). Rateizzazione and SDD pre-notifications are modelled as future-dated items (HYPOTHESIS that this is worse in Italy, PP-§G I-11).
- *Copy:* "Questo movimento era in attesa e ora è contabilizzato (stessa operazione, data aggiornata)."

### 2.11 Refunds and reimbursements

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Simplifi's Refund Tracker (mark an expected refund, track its arrival) (FACT medium, US-S101); the Actual Budget request for reimbursable tracking ("spent but not yet paid back", HSA, employer expenses, costs fronted for others) (FACT high, PP-G-61); Splitwise's `payment`/repayment model (FACT high, IT-D-01).
- **USER PROBLEM:** A refund arrives weeks later as an unexplained inflow; a friend repays you and the app shows income you never had and an expense you never made (FACT, PP-G-04, PP-§A #22).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Categorise the refund into the original category by hand (YNAB method guidance, Monarch reviewers, ASSUMPTION medium).
- **WEAKNESS:** No link between the refund and the original purchase; reimbursements inflate income; nothing tracks what is still owed.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** Automatic refund matching (same merchant, amount ≤ original, within a window) proposed in the Inbox and netted against the original purchase; a "rimborsabile" flag on any expense with an "in attesa di rimborso" ledger; partner repayments netted through the household model (2.15). Not in the first MVP cut if it slips; shipped before the household feature.
- *Copy:* "Rimborso Zalando 59,90 € collegato all'acquisto del 3 maggio. La spesa netta è ora 0 €."

### 2.12 Subscriptions and recurring (contract ledger)

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Finanzguru's contract ledger with monthly cost, next debit, notice period and one-tap cancellation (FACT medium, EU-S-35); Dyme's cancellation of >1,000 providers (FACT medium-high, EU-S-80); Rocket Money's detection and concierge (FACT, US-S71); Copilot's "already spent" treatment of recurring items at month start (FACT high / ASSUMPTION medium, US-S61/S64); Monarch's Recurring tab feeding the forecast (FACT medium, US-S32).
- **USER PROBLEM:** "Find and kill forgotten subscriptions" is a top job (FACT, PP-§B); Italian creators check renewals by hand at month-end (FACT low-medium, IT-Y-03); detection errors: one-off large payments counted as regular (Snoop), fluctuating forecasts (Bankin'), bill-detection issues (Simplifi), "I keep forgetting quarterly and yearly items" (FACT, PP-§A #20); Italian recurring costs are bimonthly (utilities), semiannual (car insurance), yearly (TARI, school), condominium fees (HYPOTHESIS for the gap, IT-§8.8).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Monthly-centric subscription lists; cancellation concierge with success fees (Rocket); switching offers as revenue (Snoop, Finanzguru).
- **WEAKNESS:** Periodicity beyond monthly is mis-detected; no notice-period awareness; monetisation incentives (fees, switching) sit inside the feature.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** A recurring engine that requires periodicity evidence (≥2 occurrences with a consistent interval and amount band) before calling anything recurring, supports monthly, bimonthly, quarterly, semiannual, annual and SDD cadences, shows next expected debit and lets the user add the notice period; recurring items are subtracted from safe-to-spend from day one (2.14); price-increase detection as an Inbox card. No cancellation concierge and no success fees in the MVP (trust ranks first); cancellation guidance (where to cancel, Italian law on recesso) as plain text.
- *Copy:* "Bolletta Enel: ogni due mesi, prossima prevista il 14 novembre (~85 €). È aumentata del 12% rispetto a luglio."

### 2.13 Insights (the monthly "AI budget" output, without the paste)

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** The Italian ChatGPT-DIY workflow: export Revolut → paste into a temporary chat → monthly budget by category, variance and three suggestions in 30 seconds (FACT low-medium, IT-Y-02); Snoop's personalised feed (FACT medium-high, EU-S-09a); Copilot's bank-fee, big-expense and credit-utilisation alerts (FACT high, US-S60); Rowan's "texts when it spots a problem" (FACT high, US-S70).
- **USER PROBLEM:** Users want the *output* (a clean monthly picture with variance and advice) with minimal effort and work around privacy to get it (FACT low-medium, IT-Y-02); "know where my money actually goes" is the second job (FACT, PP-§B).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Dashboards and charts; chat assistants over data (Monarch, Origin, Quicken Assist); SMS agents (Rowan).
- **WEAKNESS:** Charts require interpretation; chat requires a question; agents that act raise trust and regulatory issues; nothing is grounded in reconciled data, so insights repeat the errors of the ledger.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** A monthly and weekly "Cosa è successo" summary generated from the reconciled ledger: income, fixed vs variable spend, three largest variances vs the user's own baseline, subscriptions changed, transfers and savings moved, and at most three plain suggestions, each with its evidence line. Delivered as a push/notification card, readable in 30 seconds, never requiring a prompt. Nudges propose an action and ask for a one-tap decision; the product never moves money (HYPOTHESIS that messaging-first beats a chat box in Italy, US-§5.16).
- *Copy:* "Settembre: 2.150 € entrati, 1.840 € usciti. Ristoranti +38% rispetto alla tua media. Due abbonamenti sono aumentati."

### 2.14 Budgets and safe-to-spend

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Satispay's "Budget" refill envelope that creators call its most interesting feature (FACT low-medium, IT-Y-09/Y-13); PocketGuard's "In My Pocket" = income − bills − goals − budgets (FACT medium, US-S131); Simplifi's Spending Plan and Watchlists (budget a few categories without budgeting everything) (FACT medium, US-S101); Emma's "true balance" and Bankin'/Linxo's 30-day balance forecast as the most-cited daily-use features (FACT, EU-§11.5); Monarch's Flex buckets (FACT medium, US-S40).
- **USER PROBLEM:** "Tell me what is safe to spend" and "make sure I don't go overdrawn before payday" are core jobs (FACT, PP-§B); zero-based envelopes demand a mindset shift and weeks of learning (FACT, US-S13); category budgets without reconciliation produce "expenses exactly twice the actual amount" (FACT, PP-G-38).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Full envelope method (YNAB), category caps (Copilot, Revolut), one number (PocketGuard), or none (Empower).
- **WEAKNESS:** Methods are all-or-nothing; budgets built on unreconciled data are wrong; Italian users already understand a refill envelope from Satispay but competitor support across accounts requires hands-on verification.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** One number first: "Puoi spendere ancora X € fino al [payday]" computed as available balance across accounts minus recurring items not yet debited minus set-aside goals, recalculated after every sync; optional watchlists on a few categories; no envelope method in the MVP. Budget maths is only ever computed on reconciled data (transfers excluded, pending flagged), which is why 2.9 and 2.10 precede this.
- *Copy:* "Puoi spendere ancora 412 € fino al 27. Abbonamenti e bollette del mese già contati."

### 2.15 Shared and household

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Monarch's unlimited household members with separate logins and shared or individual goals, plus review assignment per member (FACT medium/high, US-S40/S28); YNAB Together (up to 6, selective sharing) (FACT high, US-S6); Spendee shared wallets (praised) (FACT, EU-S-13); Splitwise's API data model (paid/owed shares, repayments, receipts, comments with edit history) (FACT high, IT-D-01); Italian couple norms: personal accounts plus either a split app or a joint account funded equally or by income share (FACT low-medium, IT-Y-05).
- **USER PROBLEM:** Couples type every shared purchase into Splitwise, whose free tier caps adds at ~4/day with a 10-second ad (FACT medium-low, IT-V-13/V-14); Copilot has no household ("couples trap"); "shared costs fronted by one person" are invisible in PFMs (FACT, PP-§A #22).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Manual split apps; household as a paywall tier; joint accounts without contribution tracking.
- **WEAKNESS:** Manual entry duplicates bookkeeping the bank already did; privacy of personal accounts is all-or-nothing; contribution rules for the joint account are not modelled.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed; post-MVP, first expansion):** Two patterns supported natively: (a) tag a transaction on a personal account as shared with a split rule (equal, percentage, custom), settlements proposed and matched to the real repayment transfer; (b) a joint account as a household ledger with contribution tracking (equal or income-proportional). Each partner keeps a private ledger and chooses what to share; Inbox cards can be assigned to a partner. Free for a couple; never a daily cap.
- *Copy:* "Spesa condivisa 86 €: metà per Marco. Ti deve 43 €. Quando arriva il bonifico lo collego da solo."

### 2.16 Cash (and non-bank balances)

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Monefy's tap-category-amount-done entry (FACT high, EU-S-43); Hype's cash top-up at tobacconists as "the big advantage" (FACT low, IT-Y-07/Y-20); Satispay as an EMI with its own PSD2 portal (FACT, IT-§2.4); creators' broker cash and Getquin for net worth (FACT low, IT-Y-03).
- **USER PROBLEM:** Cash is >60% of Italian POS transactions by number (FACT medium-high, NEW-3) and must be typed by hand in every app (FACT low, IT-Y-04); ATM withdrawals appear as spend; Satispay and Postepay balances held by a family member and broker cash are invisible to bank apps (FACT low-medium, IT-§3.10).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** A manual cash account (YNAB, Copilot, Monarch); nothing for wallet balances; manual-only trackers for speed.
- **WEAKNESS:** ATM withdrawal and cash spend are not linked; entry friction means cash is simply ignored; wallets are treated as merchants.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** ATM withdrawal → automatically funds a cash wallet (a transfer, not a spend); one-tap cash entry (amount, category suggested from time and location permission optional); acceptance that part of spend is unobservable, shown honestly as "contanti non tracciati"; Satispay, Postepay and PayPal modelled as wallet accounts whose top-ups are transfers; manual balances for brokers with periodic reminders. Net worth as balances only in the MVP (no investment analytics).
- *Copy:* "Prelievo 100 € al bancomat: spostati nel tuo portafoglio contanti. Quando li spendi, aggiungili con un tocco."

### 2.17 Receipts

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Monarch's receipt capture by photo or e-mail forward with extraction of merchant, amount, date and line items, matched and split against the transaction (FACT high, US-S31/S32); Splitwise Pro itemisation (FACT low, IT-Y-22); Firefly users asking to import line-item splits as one transaction (FACT, PP-G-62).
- **USER PROBLEM:** A supermarket receipt mixes food, household and personal items; users want the split without typing (FACT, PP-§C, PP-G-62).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Photo attachment without extraction (Simplifi, Toshl Pro); extraction and matching only at Monarch (Oct 2025).
- **WEAKNESS:** OCR quality, cost per scan, and the Italian scontrino/fattura elettronica angle untouched.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (HYPOTHESIS; explicitly not in the MVP):** Later: photo or e-mail-forward capture matched to the transaction, line-item split into categories, with the Italian electronic invoice (fattura elettronica via the Agenzia delle Entrate cassetto fiscale) as a possible structured source for partita IVA users. Deferred because it adds cost and surface before the reconciliation core is trusted (feature count ranks last).

### 2.18 Import and export (portability)

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** YNAB's public API with `import_id` and `duplicate_import_ids`, CSV-preferred import and CSV export on every tier (FACT high, US-V1/S7); Moneyhub's CSV export at shutdown (FACT high, EU-S-08); Wallet's public API/MCP (FACT, EU-S-39).
- **USER PROBLEM:** Users have been stranded by Mint (budgets not migrated), Moneyhub, Spiir (data access ended the same day), Oval Money, the Postepay app and GoCardless (FACT, PP-§A #13); export is web-only (Simplifi), Premium-only (Rocket, contested), removed (Tricount with Premium, Fintonic since Aug 2025 per reviews) (FACT, PP-§A #15); the YNAB-Italy and UniCredit-card gaps need a file fallback (FACT/ASSUMPTION, PP-§G I-1/I-8).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** CSV import for migrations (Mint-format importers), export gated by tier or platform.
- **WEAKNESS:** Portability is treated as a churn risk rather than a trust feature; file imports create duplicates against synced data.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** validated CSV import for supported formats, with PDF parsing Later (including the ones PSD2 cannot reach), with deterministic import IDs that de-duplicate against synced items; CSV export of everything (transactions, pairs, rules, categories) on every tier, every platform; a documented read API later; account deletion that confirms what was erased. Portability is a trust feature under GDPR (priority 1 and 7), not a Plus perk.
- *Copy:* "I tuoi dati sono tuoi: esporta tutto in CSV quando vuoi, da qualsiasi piano."

### 2.19 Privacy dashboard

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** YNAB and iBear's "credentials never touch our servers" (FACT, US-S1, EU-S-41b); Finanzguru's BaFin registration and German servers as a selling point (FACT, EU-S-82/S-24); Moneyhub's "we don't sell your data" (FACT low-medium, EU-S-07a); the Italian creator who had to tell viewers "my bank confirmed it is read-only" (FACT low-medium, IT-Y-02).
- **USER PROBLEM:** Fear of sharing credentials or write access; of being charged or acted upon without consent (Rocket, Cleo); of data being sold (Snoop discloses it); of the app vanishing with the history; of opaque AI (FACT, PP-§E); fresh Italian events: Revolut's September 2026 disclosure of documents and histories to impersonators (680 clients cited in Parliament) and the Garante's €12.5M fine on Poste's apps (FACT medium, IT-W-08).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** A privacy policy; a line on the consent screen; nothing in-product.
- **WEAKNESS:** Trust claims are not verifiable by the user; disclosure processes are invisible; data residency and processors are buried.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** An in-app "I tuoi dati" page listing: which licensed AISP holds each connection and its licence number, read-only scope, consent expiry per bank (2.3), what Lilleri stores and for how long, which processors see what (aggregator, hosting region in the EU, model provider if any, with the statement that no raw transactions are sent outside the EU), that no data is sold, how legal requests are handled, and the export and delete buttons. Written in Italian at a reading level a non-expert accepts. Shipped in the MVP; it is the brand's "calm competence" made concrete (BR-§8.3).
- *Copy:* "Chi vede i tuoi dati: tu; [AISP] per leggere i movimenti (solo lettura, licenza n. …); Lilleri, su server nell'Unione Europea. Nessuno compra i tuoi dati."

### 2.20 Pricing and free tier

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Finanzguru's generous free core (multibanking, categorisation, contracts, cancellation) serving >3M users with Plus at €4.29/≈€2.99 (FACT, EU-S-68/S-69); YNAB's 34-day trial without card (FACT); Moneyhub's £1.49 loved for value (FACT low-medium); Italian ladders: Satispay €3.99/€9.99/€39.99, Hype €2.90/€9.90, Plum IT €3.99/€9.99, N26 €4.90/€9.90/€16.90 (FACT low-medium to high, IT-§2, EU-S-66); the 2026 EU Plus band €3.99–€5.99 monthly / €30–€48 annual (FACT, EU-§8).

- **WEAKNESS:** Gating correctness blocks the corrections that make the product good; short trials expire before value; the second revenue rail is either absent (and the company exits) or misaligned (lending, data sale).
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION):** Lilleri Gratis / Lilleri Plus follow `docs/business/business-model.md` and `pricing-analysis.md`: a cost-bounded free source allowance with all corrections, rules, privacy and export available; Plus value and prices are unvalidated hypotheses. The proposed 30-day non-renewing preview requires explicit purchase checkout to begin charging; the free beta has no subscription/trial. Lilleri Famiglia is Later after sharing, consent and isolation exist. Offers are outside the closed beta and require a separate opt-in; offer revenue is zero in the launch model. No data sale or lending.

### 2.21 Notifications and anomaly alerts

- **SOURCE OF INSPIRATION:** Copilot's configurable alerts (bank fee, big expense, budget, credit utilisation, payday) (FACT high, US-S60); Fintonic's alerts on fees, duplicate direct debits and overdrafts (FACT low-medium, EU-S-18); Snoop's bill-spike alerts (FACT medium-high, EU-S-09a); Emma's breach alerts (FACT medium, EU-S-02).
- **USER PROBLEM:** "Don't go overdrawn before payday", duplicate direct debits, price increases, unusual merchants (FACT, PP-§B, PP-§A #20).
- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Threshold rules (Monarch), marketing claims about "unusual charges" without a mechanism (Copilot), SMS agents (Rowan).
- **WEAKNESS:** Noise, or silence; anomaly logic undocumented.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** Few, explainable alerts, each with its evidence: projected balance below zero before the next income; a direct debit charged twice; a recurring item that increased; a new merchant above a threshold; a bank fee. All default-on but each individually mutable; delivered as Inbox cards and optional push. No "AI anomaly" wording.
- *Copy:* "Addebito SDD Fastweb presente due volte oggi (29,90 € + 29,90 €). Vuoi segnarlo come duplicato o contestarlo alla banca?"

### 2.22 Net worth and non-bank value (light)

- **CURRENT MARKET SOLUTION:** Full investment analytics (Empower, Monarch Plus with Morningstar) or nothing.
- **WEAKNESS:** Over-built for the job; wrong when transfers are unpaired.
- **OUR IMPROVEMENT (DECISION proposed):** A balances snapshot across connected and manual accounts (incl. wallets and cash) with monthly history; transfers never move net worth; giacenza media at 31 December computed for ISEE needs later (HYPOTHESIS, IT-§8.5). No portfolio analytics in the MVP.

## 5. Decisions / Recommendations

1. **DECISION (proposed): MVP core = areas 2.1–2.10, 2.12 (detection), 2.14, 2.16 (minimal), 2.18, 2.19, 2.20, 2.21; post-MVP first expansion = 2.11, 2.15, 2.22 (giacenza media), then 2.13 depth and 2.17.** Rationale: §3 ranking and the priority order (trust, correctness, security, simplicity, automation, reliability, privacy).
2. **DECISION (proposed): every automatic action carries a reason and an undo, and every uncertain action becomes an Inbox card.** This is the amended promise from `market-analysis.md` §8.2 ("si organizzano da sole — e tu vedi sempre perché") turned into a design rule.
3. **DECISION (proposed): explicit rules win over the learned model; rules are proposed from corrections and are free on every tier.** Rationale: 2.6, 2.7; EU-§12.2.
4. **DECISION (proposed): transactions have a typed lifecycle (pending, booked, future-dated, removed-by-bank, user-deleted) and a typed semantic (regular, income, transfer leg, card settlement, refund, reimbursable, wallet top-up); pairs are never created by inventing a leg; balances default to "what the bank shows today".** Rationale: 2.9, 2.10; US-V4; PP-G-24.
5. **DECISION (proposed): consent, sync health and coverage are user-facing objects with Italian copy** (per-bank expiry, cause, next retry, account-type coverage). Rationale: 2.2–2.4.
6. **DECISION (proposed): the Italian merchant and payment-type dictionary is a product asset from day one**: schema, seed list (F24, MAV/RAV, PagoPA/CBILL, bollettini, SDD, ricarica Postepay/Satispay, top supermarket and utility descriptors) and a confirmation loop that grows it.
7. **DECISION: commercial policy follows §2.20 and the business documents.** No separate price ladder or automatic trial conversion is authorised by this research; the beta has no paid billing flow.
8. **RECOMMENDATION: validate three HYPOTHESES with 20–30 S1/S2 users before Phase 2 design freezes**: (a) the empty Inbox as the hero moment versus a dashboard; (b) safe-to-spend as the first number versus category totals; (c) acceptance of automatic pairing with undo versus confirm-first.
9. **RECOMMENDATION: measure the product by correctness, not by features** — pairing precision/recall on a labelled Italian sample, categorisation acceptance rate after N confirmations, share of syncs with a named cause, consent renewals completed before expiry, minutes per month in the app (target under 5).
10. **RECOMMENDATION: write the ADRs for items 2–6 before the vertical slice** (Phase 3); decisions without an ADR are not decisions (`docs/README.md`).
